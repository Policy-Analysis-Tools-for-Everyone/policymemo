# Student beta study: Practice as software

**Practice as software: exploring the use of AI in policy reasoning and critical thinking.** The research plan for the student beta of policymemo.ai on IIPP0010 at UCL. Version 1.1, 8 October 2026, as submitted to UCL Research Ethics.

This folder holds the plan and the documents students see. **It holds no participant data and never will.** Consent records, seminar work, shared conversations and memos are kept on UCL systems only. See [How this relates to `evals/`](#how-this-relates-to-evals).

## Research question

**How does students' policy reasoning change as they move between independent work and policymemo.ai, and under what conditions does that interaction strengthen or weaken independent analytical judgement?**

1. What changes in students' problem framing, evidence use, treatment of uncertainty, options and judgements after using the tool, and do those changes last into later work?
2. When and how do students accept, adapt, reject or contest the tool's reasoning?
3. What signs are there of the tool substituting for students' own reasoning, such as adopting its wording without stated reasons?

This is a small, descriptive study. With no comparison group, it does not claim the tool caused any change; it describes what happens and under what conditions.

**Why it matters.** Students and public servants already use AI tools for policy analysis, mostly without guidance. The worry in teaching is that polished AI output replaces the judgement policy education is meant to build. There is little evidence about what actually happens to students' reasoning in real teaching. The findings will inform how AI is used in policy teaching, and how tools like policymemo.ai are designed.

## Participants

- **Who:** students on IIPP0010 in Jack Strachan's 2 seminar groups, about 11 students each. Up to 22 participants, all adults on a postgraduate programme.
- **Why that number:** the whole of both groups. The aim is to understand change in detail, not to estimate effects, so there is no calculated sample size. Results are reported as counts of students, never percentages.
- **Inclusion:** enrolled in one of Jack's 2 groups, aged 18 or over, and consenting through the study consent form.
- **Exclusion:** students in other seminar groups, and anyone who does not consent. They still use the tool and take part in every seminar.
- **Screening:** the consent form asks for name, UCL email and seminar group. Naeha checks these against the group lists.

## Roles and power

- David is faculty lead for IIPP0010 and marks the course, including live presentations, so he will know whose work he is marking. Jack teaches the seminars. Some assessment, such as live presentations and group work, cannot be marked anonymously.
- David and Jack built policymemo.ai together, and will analyse the data and co-author the outputs.
- **The safeguard:** no one who teaches or marks on IIPP0010 knows who has taken part until marks are finalised, and no one on the research team sees study material before then. Naeha holds everything until marks are finalised, then de-identifies it. The plan does not claim the team will not recognise students' work.
- Students can send critical views about the tool through Naeha. The analysis plan is lodged on the Open Science Framework before analysis, and 2 PGTAs second-code a sample, to limit bias from the team's interest in the tool.

## How students are approached

**Access to the tool is separate from the study.**

- The Student access button on [policymemo.ai/beta](https://policymemo.ai/beta) goes to a Microsoft Forms access form: name, UCL email, which AI tool. Its confirmation message gives the set-up instructions. It asks nothing about the study.
- After approval, Naeha emails all students in Jack's 2 groups with the information sheet and a link to a separate consent form in Microsoft Forms, in UCL's Microsoft 365. She owns the form and holds the responses. Jack does not recruit; the study may be mentioned in a seminar by David or Naeha.
- Students have until the end of the autumn term to decide. Naeha sends one reminder a week after the first email, and a short mid-term reminder that students can withdraw or change their choices.
- No reimbursements or incentives.

## Consent

- Students read the information sheet, then tick 10 required confirmations to take part, then any of 5 optional items:
  1. seminar work
  2. shared conversations with the tool
  3. the final memo
  4. anonymised findings informing the tool's development
  5. anonymised quotation in publications
- An unticked optional item means no consent for that part. Submitting records consent with a timestamp, and students receive a copy of their answers by email.
- Consent is taken before any data is collected. Seminar work only counts from the date a student consents.
- Consent stays valid because sharing a conversation is a fresh choice each time, the final memo is used only if ticked, and if anything about the study changes, students get the updated information sheet and confirm again before new material is used.

## Data

| Source | What it is |
|---|---|
| Seminar work | Written work students produce in seminars as part of the teaching. No extra research tasks. Copies go to Naeha, who keeps consenting students' work only |
| Conversations with the tool | Shared by choice. Students copy or export the conversation into a document, remove their name, account name and email, and upload it to Naeha through a UCL OneDrive file request. Never sent to Jack or David |
| Final memo | Obtained by Naeha after marks are finalised, for students who agreed. Cannot affect marks |
| Teaching notes | Jack's notes on how groups worked. They do not name or describe individual students |
| Consent responses | Name, UCL email, seminar group and consent choices, held by Naeha |
| Analysis notes | Coding and notes the team produce from pseudonymised material |

There are no interviews, questionnaires, audio or video recordings, or photographs. No marks or demographic data. No standardised tests.

**Personal data.** Contact details and consent choices, plus whatever indirectly identifying detail appears in students' work: a Personal Policy Problem can draw on a student's workplace or experience, and exports can include account names. The study seeks no special category data. A student's topic could incidentally touch on areas such as politics or health; students are told not to include personal or sensitive information, and any that appears is removed during pseudonymisation and not analysed. No personal data is transferred outside UCL.

## Withdrawal

- **During data collection:** email Naeha at any time, without a reason. No one who teaches or marks is told. Naeha stops collecting their material and asks whether to delete or keep what she has; if they don't say, it is deleted. Students can also withdraw from one part only.
- **After data collection:** up to **31 March 2027**, by emailing Naeha. She uses the key to find and delete all of that student's material, including anything already in analysis.
- **Limits:** after 31 March 2027, de-identified material may already be combined in the analysis. Published findings and quotations cannot be recalled. Once the key is deleted, no material can be traced to anyone.

## Storage, access and retention

**Storage.** Consent responses in Microsoft Forms in UCL's Microsoft 365. Everything else in UCL OneDrive or SharePoint. Nothing on personal devices, USB drives or personal cloud accounts, in this repository, or in any AI tool for analysis. Folders are restricted to named UCL accounts behind UCL single sign-on and multi-factor authentication, shared by named-person permissions only, never by open links. Students upload through a OneDrive file request, so they cannot see anything else in the folder. OneDrive and SharePoint encrypt files in storage and in transit.

**Access.** Until marks are finalised, only Naeha can access study material. She then pseudonymises it, keeping the consent records and the key linking codes to names in a separate folder only she can access. From then on, David and Jack work with pseudonymised research data only, and Juan Pablo and Maria with a pseudonymised sample only. No one on the research team ever sees identifiable data. After the project ends, David holds the pseudonymised data. Jack's access ends when the project ends or his UCL account closes, whichever is sooner.

Deletion means permanently removing files and folders from OneDrive and SharePoint, including the recycle bin, and deleting the form responses. Naeha deletes the identifying data and David the pseudonymised data, each confirming by email to the other.

**Lawful basis:** performance of a task in the public interest. Consent is sought as a matter of research ethics.

**Data sharing:** none. Students' work may never be fully anonymous in a small cohort, and participants have not consented to sharing with other researchers.

## Analysis

- Qualitative. Students' work is compared before and after using the tool for changes in problem framing, evidence use, treatment of uncertainty, options and judgement.
- Shared conversations show which changes followed something the tool said, and where students accepted, adapted or rejected it.
- Final memos show whether changes carried through to assessed work.
- Juan Pablo and Maria second-code a sample.
- The analysis plan is lodged with a timestamp on the Open Science Framework before analysis begins.
- Results are reported as counts of students, and include where the tool did not help as well as where it did.

## Benefits, risks and burden

**Benefits.** Little cost to participants, who use work they produce anyway. Some may find it useful to reflect on their reasoning and AI use. Teachers learn when an AI tool supports students' thinking and when it gets in the way. Wider evidence on AI and judgement helps universities and governments use these tools well.

**Why it is low risk.** Adult participants, ordinary teaching work, no extra tasks, no recordings, no sensitive topics sought, no deception.

| Risk | How it is managed |
|---|---|
| Pressure because the researchers teach and mark | Consent with Naeha; no one who teaches or marks knows who took part until marks are finalised; access separate from consent; participation invisible in the room |
| Conflict of interest: the team built the tool | Roles declared; critical views through Naeha; OSF plan; second coders |
| Privacy | Students told not to enter personal or sensitive information and to redact before sharing; Naeha removes anything left; quotations checked for identifiable topics |
| Third-party AI platforms | UCL's Microsoft Copilot licence as the default; the information sheet says consumer tiers of other tools may train on inputs |
| Justice | Non-consenting students, and those who prefer not to use AI, get the same teaching; peer review replaces the tool for anyone who wants it |
| Academic integrity | The module's rules on AI in assessment apply unchanged; the final memo is used only with consent, after marking |
| A sensitive topic, or a disclosure of harm | The study analyses reasoning, not personal detail; identifying detail is removed; any disclosure follows UCL's student support and safeguarding routes |

No foreseeable risks to the research team. No insurance confirmation needed.

## Sharing findings

- **With participants:** a short plain-language summary, emailed by Naeha at the end of the project, and shared with the module team. No individual feedback.
- **Wider:** journal articles, conference presentations, reports and blog posts, including IIPP and UCL webpages. Only anonymised findings, and short anonymised quotations from students who agreed, each checked for identifiable topics. No funder or partner restrictions on publication.

## How this relates to `evals/`

The study and the evals ask different questions. `evals/` asks what policymemo.ai does and how it should change. The study asks what happens to students' reasoning when they use it. Keep them apart:

- **No participant data enters this repository.** Not in `raw/beta/`, not in the wiki, not anywhere.
- **Product feedback from students is separate.** It comes through the student channel described in `../README.md`, is not research data, and is not used in the study.
- **Study findings reach the product only as anonymised findings, never students' own words**, and only from students who ticked optional item 4. A finding with a product implication becomes a prompt in `../wiki/prompts/` and follows the normal evals loop. It never edits a skill directly.
- **Nothing from this folder goes into a skill, a Project, a system prompt or memory**, as with the rest of `evals/`.
